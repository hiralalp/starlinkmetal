Ball Valves

Ball valves give tight, quick quarter-turn shut-off with low pressure drop. We supply floating and trunnion mounted ball valves in stainless steel and carbon steel for process, utility and pipeline service.

A ball valve uses a rotating ball with a bore through the centre. Turning the handle a quarter turn lines the bore up with the pipe to open the valve, or turns it across the pipe to close it. The result is fast, tight shut-off with very little pressure drop when the valve is open.

In a floating ball valve the ball is held between two seats and is pushed against the downstream seat by line pressure, which gives the seal. This design is simple and economical and is widely used up to medium sizes and pressures. In a trunnion mounted valve the ball is supported by shafts at the top and bottom, so the seats carry less load. This keeps operating torque low in larger sizes and higher pressure classes.

Full-bore ball valves give the same flow area as the pipe and allow pigging and unrestricted flow, while reduced-bore valves are lighter and more economical where a small pressure drop is acceptable. Three-way ball valves in L-port and T-port form can divert or mix flow with a single valve.

We supply ball valves in stainless steel (CF8, CF8M, forged F304 and F316), carbon steel (WCB, forged A105) and low-temperature steel (LCB), with PTFE, reinforced PTFE or PEEK seats, in sizes and pressure classes to suit your line.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between floating and trunnion ball valves?

In a floating valve the ball floats between the seats and is pressed to the downstream seat by the fluid. In a trunnion valve the ball is fixed on shafts, which lowers torque and wear and suits larger sizes and higher pressures.

Can a ball valve be used for throttling?

Ball valves are designed for fully open or fully closed use. Holding them partly open for long periods can damage the soft seats, so use a globe or needle valve where regulation is needed.

Which seat material should I choose?

PTFE suits most chemicals and moderate temperatures. Reinforced PTFE or PEEK handles higher pressure and temperature. Tell us your media and temperature and we will advise.
