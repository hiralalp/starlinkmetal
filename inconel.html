Inconel Alloys

Inconel nickel-chromium alloys keep their strength and resist oxidation and corrosion at extreme temperatures. We supply Inconel in pipes, tubes, sheets, plates, bars, fittings, flanges and fasteners.

Inconel is the trade name for a family of nickel-chromium superalloys. They are chosen for conditions that destroy ordinary steels: very high or very low temperatures, strongly corrosive chemicals, high pressures and oxidising atmospheres. Many Inconel grades stay strong and corrosion resistant above 1000 degrees Celsius.

Inconel 600 is a general-purpose nickel-chromium alloy for heat and corrosion resistance. Inconel 601 adds aluminium for outstanding oxidation resistance. Inconel 617 keeps its strength at very high temperature and is used in gas turbines. Inconel 625 is strengthened by niobium and molybdenum, resists seawater and many acids, and is one of the most widely used grades. Inconel 690 has high chromium and is used for nitric acid and steam generators. Inconel 718 and X-750 are precipitation hardened and reach very high strength.

These alloys are demanding to cut and weld, so it is important to buy material to the right specification and with proper documentation.

We supply Inconel pipes, tubes, sheets, plates, round bars, buttweld and forged fittings, flanges and fasteners, in the grades listed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes Inconel different from stainless steel?

Inconel is nickel-based, not iron-based, so it keeps its strength and corrosion resistance at much higher temperatures and in harsher chemicals.

Which Inconel grade is the most widely used?

Inconel 625 is among the most widely used because of its strength and wide corrosion resistance.

Is Inconel magnetic?

Most Inconel grades are non-magnetic or only slightly magnetic.

Is Inconel hard to machine?

Yes. It work-hardens and needs rigid setups, sharp tools and slow speeds.
