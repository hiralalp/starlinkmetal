Specialty & PH Stainless Steel

Beyond the common 304 and 316 grades, we supply precipitation-hardening, heat-resistant, martensitic, ferritic and high-alloy stainless steels for demanding applications.

Most people know stainless steel as 304 and 316. But there is a wide family of other stainless steels, each designed for a particular problem: higher strength, resistance to heat, resistance to wear, easier machining or better corrosion resistance. Picking the right one can make a part last many times longer.

Stainless steels fall into groups by their crystal structure. Austenitic grades such as 904L, 309, 310S and 314 are tough and non-magnetic, and the heat-resistant ones keep their strength at very high temperatures. Ferritic grades such as 409, 430 and 446 are magnetic and cost less, with good resistance to oxidation. Martensitic grades such as 410, 416, 420, 431 and 440C can be hardened by heat treatment for strength and wear resistance. Precipitation-hardening grades such as 15-5 PH and 17-4 PH reach high strength through a simple ageing treatment.

Nitronic 50 and Nitronic 60 are nitrogen-strengthened austenitic grades. Nitronic 50 offers roughly twice the yield strength of 316 with better corrosion resistance, while Nitronic 60 has outstanding resistance to galling and wear, which makes it useful for fasteners, valve stems and pump parts that slide against each other.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does PH mean in 17-4 PH?

PH stands for precipitation hardening. The steel is strengthened by a heat treatment that causes fine particles to form inside the metal.

Is 904L better than 316L?

904L has more nickel, chromium, molybdenum and copper, so it resists acids and pitting better than 316L. It is also more expensive.

Are martensitic grades magnetic?

Yes. 410, 416, 420, 431 and 440C are magnetic and can be hardened by heat treatment.

What is galling?

Galling is a kind of wear in which sliding metal surfaces stick and tear. Nitronic 60 is designed to resist it.
