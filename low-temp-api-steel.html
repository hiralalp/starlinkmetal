Carbon, Low-Temp & API 5L Steel

We supply carbon steel for general service, low-temperature steel for cold applications and API 5L line pipe for oil, gas and water pipelines.

Carbon steel is the most widely used piping material in the world. It is strong, economical and easy to weld. For hot service, grades such as ASTM A106 Grade B and C seamless pipe and A105 forged flanges and fittings are standard. For cold service, ordinary carbon steel becomes brittle, so special low-temperature steels are used instead.

ASTM A333 pipe Grade 6 is a carbon-manganese steel impact tested down to minus 45 degrees Celsius, and Grade 3 is a nickel steel for service down to about minus 100 degrees. ASTM A350 LF2 is the matching forged steel for flanges and fittings in low-temperature service. These grades are used in LNG, LPG, ethylene, ammonia and refrigeration plants and in cold-climate pipelines.

API 5L is the specification for line pipe used to carry oil, gas and water in pipelines. Grade B is the basic grade, and the X grades, X42, X52, X60, X65, X70 and X80, are higher-strength steels, where the number is the specified minimum yield strength in thousands of PSI. Higher grades allow thinner walls and lighter, more economical pipelines.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does X52 mean in API 5L?

The number is the minimum yield strength in thousands of PSI, so X52 has a minimum yield strength of 52,000 PSI.

What temperature can A333 Grade 6 be used at?

It is impact tested for service down to about minus 45 degrees Celsius.

What is A350 LF2?

A forged carbon steel for flanges and fittings, tested for low-temperature toughness.

Is A106 the same as A53?

No. A106 is seamless pipe for high-temperature service, while A53 covers seamless and welded pipe for general use.
