Nimonic Alloys

Nimonic nickel-chromium alloys are developed for high-temperature strength and creep resistance, particularly in gas turbines. We supply Nimonic 75 and Nimonic 80A.

Nimonic is a family of nickel-chromium superalloys developed in Britain for the first jet engines, and still used in gas turbines and hot sections of engines today. They keep their strength and resist creep and oxidation at temperatures well above 800 degrees Celsius.

Nimonic 75 (UNS N06075) is a nickel-chromium-titanium alloy that is easy to form and weld, and has good oxidation resistance. It is used for sheet and fabricated parts such as combustion chambers, furnace parts and exhaust systems. Nimonic 80A (UNS N07080) is precipitation hardened using titanium and aluminium, giving high creep strength at about 700 to 815 degrees Celsius. It is used for turbine blades, bolts, discs and exhaust valves.

These alloys are specialist materials and need careful handling and heat treatment. Please discuss your specification, form and quantity with us.

We supply Nimonic 75 and 80A in sheets, plates, bars and, on request, tubes and forgings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Nimonic used for?

Mostly for parts that operate at high temperature, such as gas turbine combustion chambers, blades and exhaust valves.

What is the difference between Nimonic 75 and 80A?

Nimonic 75 is a solid-solution alloy that is easy to fabricate. Nimonic 80A is age hardened and much stronger at high temperature.

Is Nimonic similar to Inconel?

Both are nickel-chromium superalloys. Nimonic alloys were developed mainly for jet engines.

Which forms are available?

Sheets, plates and bars. Other forms on request.
